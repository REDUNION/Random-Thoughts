I actually think your project has reached a point where it should stop being thought of as "a trading bot"
and start being treated as a distributed software platform. 
Looking at everything we've discussed over the past months—DuckDB/TimescaleDB, RL, NATS, FastAPI, PyQt, AI, backtesting, 
VPS deployment—I would redesign it as a collection of independent services communicating through NATS rather than as one large application. 
That architecture will make it much easier to add new strategies, brokers, AI components, or even additional machines in the future.


Based on everything we've discussed over the past months, I wouldn't write another line of trading logic yet.

Your biggest challenge is no longer how to trade—it's how to build a platform that can evolve for years. 
Every time we've solved one problem (RL observations, WebSocket integration, CSV vs. DuckDB, AI validation), 
another architectural limitation appeared. That's a sign the foundation needs to come first.

My biggest recommendation
If I were acting as the lead architect on Aether, I would treat it as an operating system for trading, not as a trading application.
The strategies become plugins.
The AI becomes a plugin.
The broker adapters become plugins.
Even the backtester becomes another service that speaks the same event protocol as live trading.
That means a strategy shouldn't know whether it's running against:

historical data,
paper trading,
live markets,
or a simulation.

It simply receives market events and emits trading decisions. 
Everything else—the data source, execution, storage, and monitoring—is handled by the platform. 
This separation will make Aether easier to extend, test, and maintain as it grows.
